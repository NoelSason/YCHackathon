# How to win this hackathon

Researched on Nov 7, 2026. Neither Naïve nor YC has published a scoring rubric or weights, so the
event's own statements rank first and the general advice ranks below them. `README.md` applies
this research to our plan.

## 1. What this event says it judges (highest weight)

From Naïve's hackathon page:

> **Build an autonomous company. Not a demo of one.** … By demo time it should be operating
> without you: **making, selling, shipping, earning.**

- **Autonomy.** "Judges look at how much of the company runs without you." That's the main score.
- **Proof that the company operated.** Show what the agents delivered, what they decided, and
  where we stepped in. Show it, don't describe it.
- **Shape.** "A CEO and a team, each with its own computer, inbox, money and the web. You set the
  goal; they make the calls."
- **Format.** A live demo of **2–4 minutes** to a small panel. The **top 8–10 teams demo again on
  the main stage.** Plan one 3-minute demo that works in both rounds.
- **Prize tracks.** 1st to 3rd overall (1st gets a guaranteed YC interview), Most autonomous
  company, Most likely to become a unicorn, Best use of Naïve, People's choice, Best design, and
  Best Mobile Automation (Mobilerun). Most track definitions aren't published.
- **Mobile track.** Mobilerun: "demonstrating a working end-to-end workflow, not just an agent
  that explains what it would do."

## 2. What recent winners of YC-hosted hackathons had in common

- **Numbers, not claims.** Winners of YC's Sep 2026 hackathon reported measured results, such as
  "cost per ticket fell 66% across 400 tickets" and "0/30 → 29/30 on held-out changes."
- **Deep use of the sponsor stack.** At that event, 90 of 91 submissions used a sponsor tool, and
  every grand-prize winner built on one. Using one is the baseline. Winning takes using it
  centrally and well.
- **Output judges can check themselves.** Judges "could assess the output directly rather than
  relying on credentials or theoretical explanations."
- **Outside attention counts.** A winning project's post drew about 382,000 views.

Team research on 16 winners of YC-hosted hackathons (Browser Use, Conversational AI, Voice Agents,
AgentMail, Agentic Payments, RL, Greptile, Own Your Intelligence, and others) found six patterns.
The per-event details came from that research and weren't re-checked here.

1. **The idea fits in one sentence.** "Two browser agents fight each other."
2. **The demo shows a state change.** Something gets found, fixed, generated, sent, bought, or
   deployed on screen.
3. **The agent acts instead of advising.**
4. **There's evidence.** Traces, counts, and before-and-after numbers, not "imagine if."
5. **Sponsor tools make the magic moment.** Each integration is a working part of one workflow,
   not a badge.
6. **The team plays to the judging.** They pick the tracks they can win and split the work early.

Their formula: **clarity × autonomy × real-world consequence × proof.** One reported rubric
(Browser Use, Feb 2026) weighted impact at 40%, and creativity, technical difficulty, and the demo
at 20% each.

**The core principle: don't demo the software. Demo the company's history since we stopped
touching it.**

| Other teams say | We show |
|---|---|
| "Our agents could make levels." | The levels they made, each tested hundreds of times |
| "Our agents could find customers." | The studios they found, and the reason each was picked |
| "Our agents could sell." | The email thread, and the Stripe payment |
| "Our agents run autonomously." | The hours of decisions they made while we slept |

## 3. What judges say in general

- A simple project with a clear, confident demo beats a complex one with a confusing demo.
- Cut features to keep the demo short. Remove every place it could stall, and pre-load data.
- Be plain about what works and what doesn't. Judges notice when you talk around missing pieces.
- Keep a recorded backup of the same demo.
- Judges revisit repos. A README with a screenshot, a live link, and the numbers settles ties.

## 4. YC's pitch rules (from YC's Demo Day guide)

- **First sentence: what we do, in plain words.**
- **Don't bury the lead.** Open with the best real number.
- **Three or four points at most**, then repeat them at the end.
- **No exaggeration.** YC calls it a "fatal error." Say "we have zero revenue" if that's true.
- **No jargon.** "Agents" is fine. "Multi-agent orchestration substrate" isn't.
- For "Most likely to become a unicorn," show bottom-up math (how many customers, how much each
  pays), not a top-down market size.
